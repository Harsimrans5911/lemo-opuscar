# The Voyage Home — treatment

Watercolor Brush · vertical 1080×1920 · 61.2 s · 30 fps · English voice and subtitles

The times in the tables below are the plan. The finished film follows `timeline.js`: 66.7 bpm, one bar = 3.6 s, 17 bars, with the voice lines at their measured lengths. No voice-over was dropped and no storyboard stop was requested.

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. The crossing as a transect.** One lateral walk from the Oregon sawmill, across the Pacific, to Calcutta. Clear, but it is the obvious shape for this style and a sideways journey wastes a vertical frame.
- **B. The traveller's bundle.** A locked specimen plate: the few things he carries (a prayer book, a newspaper, a ticket), each with a note. Good for "what he believes", but static, and it hides the arrest, which is the turn.
- **C. One tall sheet, read downward, opened by a question.** A hand-written question on blank paper ("Why would a free man sail home to a prison?"). The camera then tilts down a single tall page: the mill, the insult, the party, what he bows to, the ocean, the harbour, the bars. The answer is written at the bottom.

**Chosen: C.** The reel is vertical, so the journey runs down the page; the question is the hook and the answer is the bookend; the inner motive (faith, teacher, insult) sits in the middle of the sheet, between the mill and the sea, where the decision is made.

Opening image: a hand-written question. Ending: the answer in the same hand, under a lamp that is still burning. Score: a plucked string over a drone that drops out for the arrest and returns a tone brighter.

## 2. Logline and arc

A Punjabi mill worker in Oregon, president of the Ghadar Party, sails home in 1914 to free India, is arrested at the dock, and is not broken by sixteen years in prison.

- **Setup**: Oregon. Work, the insult, the party.
- **What he bows to**: no idol; the Guru's word, a teacher, a lamp.
- **Turn**: he sails; the British are waiting at Calcutta; bars are painted over him.
- **Ending**: the bars wash away; the lamp is still lit; he lives to see India free.

## 3. Benchmark

- A naturalist's field journal page (specimen and note): learn the calm of one subject per view with a hand note; do not take any layout or drawing.
- Painted title cards of historical documentaries that hold on one object while the voice carries the fact: learn the restraint (one image per sentence, long holds); do not take shots, type or music.

## 9. Type and subtitles

- Subtitles: Cormorant Garamond italic, ink at 78 % over a paper halo, centred, two lines at most, kept above Instagram's bottom interface (baseline zone at about 74 % of the height).
- Hand notes and the question and answer: Caveat, written left to right.
- Dates and the masthead year: IBM Plex Mono, wide tracking.
- Accent: one saffron, used for the flag, the flame, the route line and the dawn.

## 10. Compared with the style's demo

Structure (a tall sheet read downward, not a sideways walk), opening (a written question), signature shot (bars painted over the figure), camera path (vertical tilt with pushes), score (an original synthesised score, with silence at the turn) and ending (a lamp and a written answer) all differ from the demo.

## 11. Punjabi version (current cut)

The film was re-voiced in Punjabi and rebuilt around one idea: sacrifice.

- **Opening:** Guru Nanak's line is written on blank paper, "ਜਉ ਤਉ ਪ੍ਰੇਮ ਖੇਲਣ ਕਾ ਚਾਉ ॥ ਸਿਰੁ ਧਰਿ ਤਲੀ ਗਲੀ ਮੇਰੀ ਆਉ ॥" (if you would play the game of love, come with your head on your palm). The narrator speaks it in modern Punjabi.
- **Thread:** the head. He bows it only to the Guru's word; he sails "with his head on his palm"; in prison "his head never bowed".
- **Ending:** the same salok's closing line is written under the lamp, "ਸਿਰੁ ਦੀਜੈ ਕਾਣਿ ਨ ਕੀਜੈ ॥", and the narrator's last words name it: sacrifice is to give your head, but never to bow it.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs "Sufi - Wise Punjabi Conversation", Eleven v3. On-screen text and subtitles are in Gurmukhi (Tiro Gurmukhi); an English subtitle file is delivered beside the Punjabi one.
- **Timing:** 73.3 s. Picture and score are still written in score time (61.2 s, 17 bars); `WARP` in `timeline.js` stretches each scene to the length of its Punjabi line, so the tempo breathes scene by scene.
